Where Handl picks up ​

Every step above stayed inside a host language: arrows were syntax sugar in Haskell, effects were a thin layer, and the interesting structure (laws, rewrites, graded effects) was invisible to tools.

Handl inverts that. The arrow calculus isn't syntax over something else; it is the IR. Programs are 1-morphisms, laws and rewrites are 2-morphisms, and transformations are data like everything else. That gives us:

  • Inspectable computation: arrows serialize, diff, and transform.
  • Language as data: a Language is declarative syntax + grammar + eval scheme + type rules, not hardcoded engine logic.
  • Graded effects: the Graded Modal Sanada Arrow Calculus, with graded operations and handlers.
  • Interchangeable engines: lex, parse, transform, typecheck, exec (tree-walk, bytecode, Cranelift, LLVM, Triton, CUDA) all consume the same representation.

The vision: a language infrastructure where the mathematics is the substrate, and everything (tools, libraries, hosts, products) is built on top of it.
