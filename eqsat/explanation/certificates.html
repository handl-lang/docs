Certificates — the replayable derivation ​

A certificate is the artifact a search produces that outlives the search: a replayable derivation from the source term to the extracted term. The certificate, not the timing, is the claim.

The model ​

  • Certificate — the derivation: an ordered list of CertSteps (a rule name, the e-class it fired on, the resulting merge) over ProofEdges.
  • EGraph.certificate(src, dst, root_eid=...) — extract the derivation the saturated graph witnesses between two terms.
  • verify_certificate(term, cert, *, strict=False) — replay the certificate standalone: apply each step to the source term and check the claimed result lands in the same class. A certificate that does not replay raises CertificateVerificationError — no trust in the engine that emitted it.
  • cert_to_data / cert_from_data — the transport form, so a certificate can cross a wire, a cache, or a backend boundary.

Two places certificates do the work ​

  1. Delivery. Every program the pipeline lowers ships its certificate — search → extract → certify — so downstream consumers verify the derivation, not the optimizer's reputation. See your first search().
  2. Admission. The derivability check in the gauntlet is a certificate replay: verify_law(lhs, rhs, rules) (the domain side, in graphopt's mlopt_discovery) interns both sides in a fresh small e-graph, saturates under the known laws, and replays the witness in both directions — a merge is a genuine derivation, never a trusted stub. This substrate supplies the e-graph and the replay; the admission policy is the domain's.

The honest boundary ​

A certificate proves equality of the derivation, nothing else: it says the extracted program computes the same thing as the source. Whether the extraction was worth it is a measured question the certificate deliberately does not answer.
