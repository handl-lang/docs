  • Arrows, not functions

    Computation composes as arrows: inspectable structure, not closures you can only call.

  • Serializable

    Programs, laws, and effects are data: they round-trip through JSON, YAML, and any engine's memory model.

  • Globular

    Programs are 1-morphisms, laws and rewrites are 2-morphisms, and transformations extend into higher dimensions.

  • Graded effects

    Operations carry grades and handlers supply meaning: effects are algebraic and first-class.

  • Language as data

    Syntax, grammar, AST shapes, eval schemes, and type rules are declarative, not hardcoded into engines.

  • Runtime-independent

    One IR feeds tree-walk, bytecode, Cranelift, LLVM, Triton, and CUDA backends interchangeably.

The whole calculus fits in six combinators (arr, >>>, first, choice, loop, app). Everything else (programs, laws, coherence, graded effects) is built from these as data: parse >>> validate >>> exec is a structure you can inspect, rewrite, and serialize, not a closure you can only call. See the paradigm for what this buys you.

The story ​

Most languages hide their foundations. You apply functions, effects stay implicit, and the interesting structure (the laws, the rewrites, the effects) remains locked inside closures nobody can inspect.

Handl starts from the opposite choice: computation as arrows, not functions. Category theory spent decades building the mathematics for exactly this: arrows, effects, and handlers, where structure is first-class and meaning comes from interpretation rather than a hardcoded runtime. We made that mathematics the IR itself, so programs, laws, and transformations are all data you can read, rewrite, and execute.

Read the full story: from Moggi's monads to Sanada's handlers, and where Handl picks up.

The strategy ​

handl-lang is language infrastructure, not a single language: a closed arrow calculus at the bottom, with engines, runtime-agnostic libraries, curated kits, tools, and hosts built on top. Composition over application, inspectable structure over hidden closures.
