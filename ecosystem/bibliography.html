Bibliography ​

These academic works are a major inspiration for Handl. There is already a substantial body of mathematical groundwork for arrows via category theory: that is what we are drawing on.

Foundational work behind the arrow calculus and the categorical semantics the kernel is built on.

Arrows ​

  • John Hughes: Generalising Monads to Arrows (2000, Science of Computer Programming). Introduced arrows and their core combinators (arr, >>>, first).
  • Ross Paterson: A New Notation for Arrows (2001, ICFP). Formalized proc notation, algebraic laws, and categorical correspondences.
  • Sam Lindley, Philip Wadler & Jeremy Yallop: The Arrow Calculus (2010, Journal of Functional Programming). Defined a formal typed calculus and equational semantics for arrows.

Categorical semantics ​

  • Eugenio Moggi: Notions of Computation and Monads (1991, Information and Computation). Monads as notions of computation.
  • John Power & Edmund Robinson: Premonoidal Categories and Notions of Computation (1997, Mathematical Structures in Computer Science).
  • John Power & Hayo Thielecke: Closed Freyd- and κ-categories (1999, ICALP). Freyd and premonoidal categories, the canonical categorical model separating pure values from effectful computations.
  • Paul Levy: Call-by-push-value (PhD thesis, Queen Mary and Westfield College, University of London, 2001). Value/computation discipline that premonoidal models are built on.

Monads in Prof ​

  • Chris Heunen & Bart Jacobs: Arrows, like Monads, are Monoids (2006, ENTCS). Arrows as monoids in categories of bifunctors.
  • Bart Jacobs, Chris Heunen & Ichiro Hasuo: Categorical Semantics for Arrows (2009, Journal of Functional Programming). Denotational semantics via Eilenberg–Moore and Kleisli constructions ("arrows are Freyd categories").
  • Kazuyuki Asada: Arrows are Strong Monads (2010, MSFP; PDF). Identifies first with strength: arrows are strong monads (promonads) in Prof.

Higher categories ​

Handl's IR is globular and higher-categorical: programs are 1-morphisms, laws and rewrites are 2-morphisms, and transformations extend upward. These are the foundations that make "all data, all dimensions" coherent.

  • Michael A. Batanin: Monoidal Globular Categories as a Natural Environment for the Theory of Weak n-Categories (1998, Advances in Mathematics). Globular operads and weak ω-categories: the canonical setting for globular higher structure.
  • Tom Leinster: Higher Operads, Higher Categories (2004, Cambridge). The standard reference for generalized operads and n-categorical structures.
  • Eugenia Cheng & Aaron Lauda: Higher-Dimensional Categories: an Illustrated Guide Book (2004, IMA workshop notes). A friendly tour of the competing definitions of n-category.
  • Jacob Lurie: Higher Topos Theory (2009). ∞-categories via quasi-categories: the modern foundation for infinite-dimensional composition and coherence.

Graded type theory ​

Handl's graded effects descend from graded monads and graded algebraic theories: the grade on an operation is what the type-level literature calls a coeffect/grade.

  • Dominic Orchard, Vilem-Benjamin Liepelt & Harley Eades III: Quantitative Program Reasoning with Graded Modal Types (2019, PACMPL 3(ICFP), Article 110). Graded modal types as the type-level counterpart of graded effects.
  • Satoshi Kura: Graded Algebraic Theories (2020, FoSSaCS; arXiv:2002.06784). Graded algebraic and Lawvere theories, equivalent to finitary graded monads.
  • Jeff Egger, Rasmus Ejlers Møgelberg & Alex Simpson: The Enriched Effect Calculus: Syntax and Semantics (2014, Journal of Logic and Computation 24(3)). Effect calculi enriched with linear logic primitives.
  • Benjamin Moon, Harley Eades III & Dominic Orchard: Graded Modal Dependent Type Theory (2021, ESOP). Graded analysis of dataflow in a dependent type theory; grades applied to both terms and types.

Effects and handlers for arrows ​

  • Takahiro Sanada: Algebraic Effects and Handlers for Arrows (2024, Journal of Functional Programming; also ICFP 2024). Extended the arrow calculus with algebraic effects and handlers via algebras over strong promonads.
  • Takahiro Sanada, Keisuke Hoshino, Kenshin Hirai & Shin-ya Katsumata: Programming Backpropagation with Reverse Handlers for Arrows (2026, PACMPL 10(ICFP), Article 306; also arXiv:2602.18090). Modeled neural networks and backpropagation via linear reverse effect handlers, combining strong promonads with Cartesian reverse differential categories.
