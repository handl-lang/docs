Build list — what has to exist before the machine can find a kernel ​

Refactor context: lab-form per ADR-0159; ADR-0160 allocates the IR. Visibility (ADR-0150): lib-kernel-ir public; compile-triton, lib-triton, ext-opt-* private until done. Definition of done: the four split_k variants of qgemv are generated from IR terms (not written by hand), all agree with awq_gemm (cosine ≥ 0.9999), and at least one matches the hand-written kernel's cold time on down_proj (58.9 µs).

Total to M1: ~5 days.

No kit in M1. A kit assembles several libs; one lib is loaded directly. kit-kernel-ir arrives at M2, when lib-triton-qgemv (the binding), the cost tables and the base types have to meet — the same reason kit-prelude-lang exists and a lone lib does not.

Deliberately not in M1 ​

  • lib-triton-qgemv binding. Needed to ship the kernel through the platform, not to find it. Deferred until the search proves it can find one worth shipping.
  • M > 1 tiling / prefill, FP8, GPTQ layouts. Coverage work; it only makes sense once both operators are proven.
  • Compile cache and pipelining combinators. Both are required for scale (~10⁴ candidates) but not for the M1 gate (4 variants). Flagged so they are not forgotten: without a cache the search is rate-limited by Triton compilation.
  • Static cost ranking. The model is used only as a hard bound (roofline floor, legality). This week's evidence: warm-cache ranking contradicted deploy ranking on three of four shapes.

What the M1 result would settle ​

If a fixed lowering matches the hand kernel on qgemv and reaches parity on gate_proj, generalising is worth it and the search has a substrate. If it cannot, no amount of IR generality will help — a codegen that loses to 30 lines of hand-written Triton will lose at scale too, and the honest response is to keep hand-writing kernels and use the search only for configs.
