The margin, and what it means ​

Date: 2026-10-27

One line: hand-tuning is expensive and therefore only bought for popular shapes; automating it collapses the cost per experiment, and the first measured margin over a hand-written production kernel came from exactly a shape nobody had tuned.

What we actually measured ​

Against awq_gemm — vLLM's hand-written, production 4-bit CUDA kernel — on the real layers of Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct-AWQ, one token at a time:

geometrylayersoursawq_gemmresult
down_proj 4864→8962455.9 µs186.0 µs3.32× faster
q_proj/o_proj 896→8964831.738.31.21× faster
gate_proj/up_proj 896→48644860.053.50.89× (loss)
k_proj/v_proj 896→1284822.637.91.68× over vLLM, but plain matmul is 16.3 — route away

120 of 168 linears beat awq_gemm. Every number is fp32-exact to ~1e-6 against the dequantized reference.

The kernel was written in an afternoon and never hand-tuned. The configs were chosen by a measured sweep (48 combinations per shape) — the machine picked them, not a person.

Why the margin exists ​

Not because we out-thought the kernel engineers. awq_gemm is tuned for the shapes their customers actually run. A 896×4864 matmul inside a 0.5B model on a 4 GB laptop GPU is an unattended shape — it is not worth a specialist's week.

That is the whole economic argument:

Human tuning has a high fixed cost per shape, so it is only bought where the shape is popular. Automation collapses that cost, so the long tail becomes worth optimizing. The world has vastly more model shapes than any kernel team can hand-tune.

The margin is arbitrage on unattended cases, not a breakthrough of a physical ceiling. That distinction matters, because it tells you where the next margins are: everywhere nobody looked.

What scales, and what does not ​

Does not scale: per-kernel speed. Kernel time is bounded by memory bandwidth and FLOPs. The gap between "well hand-tuned" and "optimal" is typically 1.1–2×, occasionally 3–5×. No amount of search produces a 100× kernel, because the hardware is the ceiling.

Does scale: coverage. If automation optimizes 50,000 shapes instead of 50, the aggregate value is enormous even when each win is 1.3×. The exponential lives in breadth, and in how often models and formats change — not in a single miracle number.

Also scales: the cost per experiment. Seconds instead of days. That is the compounding asset; every other benefit is downstream of it.

Where novelty can come from (measured, not assumed) ​

The strongest result in this project is about where new structure enters:

armmachine-original admitted
compose rules the bank already has0 / 39
allowed to name new operations260 / 262

Novelty comes from growing the vocabulary, not from searching harder inside a fixed one. "Keep exploring" only produces new algorithms if the exploration may invent concepts. Everything else is rearrangement.

What this does not prove ​

The machine did not invent the kernel. I wrote the int4 GEMV; the machine swept its configs. So the evidence is the substrate can host and tune a kernel that beats a hand-tuned production kernel — not the loop discovers such a kernel unaided. That gap is the RL claim, and it is still open.

The model did not get faster. End-to-end on the same AWQ model in vLLM, routed per shape: 314.4 ms stock vs 317.6 ms ours (+1.0 %, 12/20 rounds, tokens identical). Flat. The kernel wins are real and the model win is not, for two known reasons:

  1. dtype conversion — the model runs fp16 and our kernel is fp32-in/fp32-out, so each call pays two copies the isolated benchmarks never saw;
  2. dilution — two of four geometries route to us; attention, sampling and scheduling dominate the rest of a token.

Both have known fixes (fp16 kernel, more coverage). Neither is done.

Precision was not matched. We accumulate fp32 and return fp32; awq_gemm is fp16 in and out. The isolated comparison flattered us, and in a real model it turned from a fairness question into a cost.

What the session also established (negatives worth keeping) ​

  • Whole Llama through vLLM: our weights are speed-neutral. Token exact, no speed. The transfer path "hand them weights" carries almost none of the optimization.
  • Fusing the MLP chain inside vLLM is worth at most 1.29 %. Measured before building it: the activation is 7.5 µs and a launch is 9.5 µs on a 3,490 µs token. The graph is already fused; there is nothing there.
  • Compute-bound shapes are capped at 1.03× by construction — an equality-preserving rewrite cannot change the FLOPs a dense model must do. 28 of 65 zoo rows sit in that regime.
  • Where the headroom is: 25 of 65 zoo rows are launch-bound, median ceiling 2.23×, max 5.95×.

What to do next, in order ​

  1. fp16 kernel (fp32 accumulate). Without it the end-to-end number cannot move, no matter how good the isolated numbers get.
  2. Let the loop propose the kernel, not just its config. That is the experiment that turns this report's optimism into evidence.
  3. Coverage: apply the same search to more shapes and formats — the long tail is the actual market.
  4. An honest referee at scale. On this laptop, run-to-run noise sometimes reached ±50 %, ten times the effect. Ten thousand experiments measured that way produce ten thousand false positives. The measurement discipline — negatives written down next to wins — is the asset that makes any of this trustworthy.

The judgment ​

The project began as a bet that looked implausible. It now has a measured, reproducible margin over a hand-written production kernel, on real weights, from a kernel nobody tuned by hand.

That is not "impossible became possible." It is narrower and more useful: the unattended corners of the ML stack are large, and the cost of attending to them just collapsed. Per-shape wins are modest (1.2–3×); the number that matters is that they can now exist everywhere.
