Proposal — a kernel IR as data, and the libs it needs ​

Status: proposed · follows 2026-10-27-proposal-int4-kernel-vocabulary.mdQuestion it answers: if a kernel body should be transparent (a term, not an opaque triton.jit blob), where does that term live and what carries it?

The rule that constrains the answer ​

The kernel's six combinators stay closed. A kernel IR is not a new set of kernel combinators — that is exactly the "cost of an open kernel" the kernel paper argues against. It enters the platform the way every other language does: as a Language (data), interpreted by engines. So the IR is a language, the search is over its terms, and nothing downstream of the kernel grows.

Nothing new is invented beyond the IR itself; every other artifact lands in a family that already exists.

The IR, kept deliberately small ​

Ten ops, each with a denotation (a torch reference, so equivalence is checkable) and a cost (bytes, ops, launches — the enrichment):

tile_load   (tensor, idx)        -> tile
unpack_i4   (packed, shift)      -> int4 tile
dequant     (q, zero, scale)     -> fp tile
dot         (a, b)               -> tile
split       (k_range, n_parts)   -> k_range
stage       (partial)            -> partial
reduce      (partials)           -> tile
store       (tile, idx)          -> ()
bias        (tile, bias)         -> tile

The test of "is the body transparent" is then mechanical: two terms are equal iff their denotations are equal. split_k = 4 becomes four split terms with provably equal composed denotation, not a parameter someone hopes is safe.

Review — what is wrong with the above ​

1. The IR as specified cannot express what makes kernels fast. The op list has tile_load, dot, split, stage, reduce — all data operations. It has no software pipelining, no accumulator reuse across K tiles, no shared-memory staging. Those are the things that separate 42 % of roofline from 70 %. A schedule language that cannot state them can only express naive schedules, which will lose to the hand kernel every time. Minimum additions: a pipeline(depth) combinator and stage_shared (a residency annotation), or the IR is decorative.

2. Denotation equality proves meaning, not schedule equivalence. Two terms with identical denotations can differ 3× in cost — that is the whole point of searching. So "certify the body" reduces to the law we already have; the IR's real payoff is search, and the review should say so instead of implying it buys proof.

3. A static cost model will prune winners. Evidence from this week: the grid search's own warm-cache ranking contradicted the deploy-regime ranking on three of four shapes. A cost model is a cruder ranking than a measurement, so it must be used only as a hard bound (roofline lower bound, legality), never to choose between feasible candidates.

4. Search is rate-limited by Triton compilation, not by ideas. 288 candidates ≈ minutes; 10⁴ candidates ≈ hours of compilation. The proposal does not mention a compile cache or a way to instantiate without a full triton.jit round trip. Without that, the IR cannot be searched at the scale where it beats hand-writing.

5. The economics are unstated. Today's result — 29 % end-to-end — came from a hand-written kernel plus a config sweep. The IR is justified only if it then covers many shapes and formats. If the plan is "a few more kernels", the IR is over-engineering; if the plan is "hundreds of shapes across GPTQ/AWQ/FP8", it is the only thing that scales. That decision should be explicit before M0.

6. Legitimacy, not just design. A new Language in the platform needs an ADR in the host repo — the kernel stays closed and the IR enters as data, which is the right shape, but it should be recorded as such or the next proposal will quietly add a combinator.

Recommended cut-down for M1 ​

Do not build the ten-op IR first. Build one op end to end: qgemv with a fixed lowering, its torch denotation, and the four split_k variants. Gate: all four denotations equal to awq_gemm (cosine ≥ 0.9999) and at least one variant matching the hand kernel's speed.

If that works, the IR earns its second op. If the fixed lowering cannot match a hand-written kernel on one op, generalising it is wasted work.

  • IR creep. Ten ops is the budget; an IR that grows into a compiler project loses to just writing Triton. Anything that cannot be expressed in these ten is a finding about the platform.
  • Lowering quality. compile-triton must reach the same code quality as the hand kernel, or the IR has bought provability at the cost of speed — measured, not assumed.
  • Residual opacity, stated once: the silicon's response (registers, bank conflicts, ptxas) is never derivable from a term. Cost stays model + measurement. That is why the referee is part of the design and not an afterthought.
