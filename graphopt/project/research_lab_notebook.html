On this page

Research lab notebook ​

The honest record of how this was found. Chronological and exploratory, not a polished report. Plain prose, first-person plural. The point of this document is that a future collaborator reads it and does not repeat the mistakes. Measured numbers are given where they matter, each traceable to a retro under retros/, a decision under adrs/, or a plan under plans/.

Read this with the paper draft (../docs/paper_draft.md) open: the paper says what we claim; this says how we got there and what it cost.

0. How to read this ​

We kept a retro per work item from early on, and the retros are the raw material — 127 of them at the time of writing, each with the measurement, the negative, and the honest limits. This notebook is the spine through them: the questions in the order we asked them, the doors that closed, the process lessons that cost real time, the reframings, and what is still open. Where a retro is the authority we name it; where we are summarising across retros we say so.

Two conventions we adopted and now hold to. First: a negative is a result — a probe that refuses to derive something, with the gap named, is written up exactly like a probe that succeeds. Second: no number without its artifact — every figure below has a retro, baseline or tool behind it.

1. The questions, in the order we asked them ​

1.1 2025-09 — "Is there structure in the weights themselves?" ​

The founding bet was weight-space compression: if trained weights hid exact structure (duplicates, shared subspaces, symmetries, generators), the equivalence machinery could shrink storage losslessly. This is the original motivation and it was the first thing we measured, on llama2.c stories15M (15 M params) and stories110M.

Every hypothesis class we could name, measured (retros/measure_weights.py; adrs/0001-weight-space-structure-falsified.md):

  • Phase 0 — Toeplitz/displacement rank: ~270–288 of 288, full rank. No exact low-rank structure.
  • Phase 0b — the structured-algebra family: Kronecker-sum beat SVD by only 15–31 %; H-matrix, sparse, monarch/butterfly, INR coordinate-fit all dead or inconclusive. The one live signal was the token embedding: rank 19 at 95 % Frobenius energy, ~15× compressible.
  • Phase 5 — real perplexity on TinyStories (baseline ppl 5.03). The embedding's top-19 directions are not the ones next-token prediction needs: every structural rank (r192 → ppl 214) killed quality. Only int8 RTN survived (4×, ppl 5.16).
  • Relational — cross-layer bitwise dups / shared subspace / Procrustes alignment: 0 hits, full column rank, 0 aligned, on both checkpoints.
  • Symmetry — equivariance WG=GW: residual ≈ √2 (the random value). No symmetry.
  • Polynomial — low-degree p(W)=0: minimal polynomial = full degree (288 distinct eigenvalues).
  • Learned displacement — rank(AW−WB) over structured A,B: displacement rank ~280/288 — full.

The door closed (ADR 0001). Trained checkpoint weights are information-dense at the bit level; no emergent exact structure exists to exploit. The lesson that outlived the measurement: a norm bound does not predict task quality. ‖ΔW‖ bounds do not predict Δppl. Certified compression is real but certifies the wrong quantity for weight space. What survived is only the architectural corner — tying, materialized GQA replication, dead params, merged adapters (measured 0–75 % by archetype; the full phase tables were migrated to docs/results_curated.md §7 when the report was retired).

The founding report also opened on a unified thesis — a model and its weights are one semantic program, so parameter storage and computation structure fall out of a single search — with parameters as Param leaves in the same term language as compute, making elimination a corollary. That half did not survive: the compute side did, the weight side was falsified here. It is recorded, with its mechanism, in docs/results_curated.md §7.9.

This is the first dead end and it set the tone: we killed our own founding bet with our own measurements and wrote it up as ADR 0001.

1.2 2026-09 — "What is the project actually about?" ​

With weight-space closed, the capabilities had grown independently — carrier monoids, non-local lifts, the morphism engine, bounded rewrites, exact weight sharing — each documented in a different place and each justified on its own. The pitch still led with "compression with a receipt" after compression had been closed. The docs were drifting.

So we wrote down one thesis and made every capability answer to it (ADR 0002): a tensor program is a morphism of a category; optimization is search in the quotient of the free category by the laws of the structures the program instantiates. Op-level compilers rewrite generators; this rewrites morphisms up to structure, which is why it reaches programs they provably cannot. Six binding rules followed — name your structure; the certificate is the artifact; construction primary, saturation fallback; exact and bounded are one object; scope claims to structure.

The rule that caused the most subsequent trouble was Rule 3: "Directed search, not learning. RL is a non-goal." That was right for reach — structure is what makes search beat enumeration — but it wrongly foreclosed policy (choosing which rewrite to fire next). ADR 0003 amended it.

1.3 2026-10 — "Is evaluation a separate thing, and is RL allowed?" ​

ADR 0003 split the pipeline into four independent dimensions — semantics / search / evaluation / execution — each answering exactly one question, with explicit ports. It amended Rule 3 to allow a learned selector over the structure while keeping the certificate constraint. The central invariant it protects: feasibility is hard, performance is soft. supported_ops is a hard bound (an unlowerable form prices at +inf); a performance model is a soft ranking that may never prune a certified alternative.

The reason RL became allowed, stated plainly: the certificate is a hard filter (accept/reject), never a soft reward. Because the learner cannot launder a wrong answer into a reward, it is free to explore aggressively. This is the formal/RL interaction the paper now leads with.

ADR 0004 ("abstraction as a legal move") opened the move set: constructed objects — folds, bridges, carriers — are admissible data, not pre-approved Python. It also recorded the first measured negative about learning: asking a learned policy to invent laws directly was ~10× worse than enumeration (retros/learned-proposal; the meta_game probe). Direct neural law invention does not scale; structured construction does.

ADR 0005 ("the open game") made the frame explicit: every rule, object, vocabulary, runtime and pricing inside the optimizer is machine-made data admitted by anchored machinery — and the only fixed pieces are the substrate (torch/GPU/Triton), the correctness oracle, and the measured objective (wall-clock, memory). A self-minted objective is a rigged race; a machine that could redefine equivalence voids every certificate. Everything else is inside the game.

2. Dead ends and closed negatives ​

These are the results we are most glad to have. Each one saved time later and each one is a measurement, not an opinion.

2.1 The anti-unification reach-null (ADR 0007 layer 1) ​

ADR 0007 proposed four layers for minting new meaning into the object language. Layer 1 — definitional atoms by anti-unification (the Babble-style library-learning move) — was the one we expected to pay.

It mints real atoms: provenance-carrying, unfoldable, verified fp64 end-to-end, and the compression is real (a deq winner is 12 ops spelled vs 26 unfolded). But on all three workloads (delta×6, deq×6, retnet T=64/K=4) the cheapest extractable member and the class count were identical with and without the atoms. Naming structure did not expand reach. The strict arm is strictly worse under tight max_nodes (intro members consume enodes with no pay). fold_ruleset found zero targets: minted motifs live in exports, not in law outputs.

Per ADR 0007's own falsification clause #1, layer 1's reach-expansion claim is measured negative on these workloads. The machinery stays as compression tooling (shorter spellings, provenance), not a reach mechanism. The retro is the ADR's own measurements section; the tool is lab/probes/anti_unify_probe.py.

2.2 The atom pay-null (the composed loop) ​

The composed loop (lab/mint/atom_synth_loop.py) runs need → handler → laws → admission → pay probe end to end. It minted one full semantic atom — scan_fold, the selective-scan recurrence h_t = a_t·h_{t-1}+b_t, verified at 1.78e-15 — with 5 certified primitive laws, plus law sets for cumsum/cumprod/logcumsumexp. It is a real end-to-end mint.

And it pays nothing. 0 certified laws fire on real terms. The minted atom computes the fold's total, while the corpus emits the sequence per step. The paying sibling (fold_emit) sits >200 k deep in the round-robin enumeration; it verifies at 6.7e-16 when reached directly — so the gap is enumeration ordering, not expressiveness. The corpus's actual cumsum usages (cumsum(mul(x,x)), cumsum(elu(k)+1), cumsum(mul(unsq,unsq))) all live in the guarded-out region: the guards that made the laws certifiable are exactly what correctly stops them firing on the real sites.

This is ADR 0007's falsification criteria working as designed: truth and pay are measured separately, and they were disjoint here (retros/atom-synth-loop.md). The retro produced the ordering fix (feat/enum-order) as a follow-up.

2.3 The BiRefNet bounded negative ​

Question: does the precision lane (error_budget=) buy headroom on a real pretrained model where the exact lane was already FLOP-neutral? Model: the real ZhengPeng7/BiRefNet HF checkpoint, 220.18 M params, Swin-v1-L + an ASPPDeformable decoder (lab/probes/birefnet_bounded_probe.py).

The bounded offers exist: 20 sites at budget 1e-2, 56 at 3e-2, certified bounds inside budget. The bound gate stayed honest. And nothing delivered: only 18 of 2304 rows dedup at ≤9.9e-3 (0.78 % of the weight), so the gather/re-expand/bias-add wrapper prices 1.32× the dense member. Launch-aware pricing correctly declined every offer. The fp32 gate stayed bitwise-clean (fresh_rel = 0.0 on every cell).

Verdict: the precision lane does not pay on BiRefNet — not because the bound machinery failed, but because the real structure is too thin to price. This is a certified negative: the machinery offered, the gate refused, and the refusal was right. Side findings worth keeping: 43 nn.BatchNorm2d modules (so conv+BN fusion targets do exist — retros/birefnet-bounded-lane.md, retros/weightspace-fold.md), and deform_conv2d exported with no lowering binding — a real coverage gap, since fixed.

2.4 The enumeration-vs-learning ledger ​

We measured the learned path against enumeration repeatedly, and the ledger is mixed — which is the honest result.

  • Direct law invention by a learned policy: ~10× worse than enumeration. Falsified, not reopened (ADR 0004).
  • The meta-arena: a cold linear REINFORCE player beats the scripted playbook (−0.03 vs −0.10 over 40 held-out boards) and produces 3 certified structural wins — but only after the claim(tag) option move fused the composed declare → handle line into one refereed action. The wins ride sampling variance, not learned preference (op:claim's weight sits ≈+0.04). Action-space structure, not the learner, was the fix (retros/meta-arena-player.md).
  • The contraction player: beats opt_einsum's randomised greedy at equal wall-clock at n=40 (0.89–1.01× across three seeds), 0.51–0.90× the deterministic greedy. Real, but small; the headroom is search cost, not quality (retros/contraction-player-shipped.md).
  • The learned prior: ranks held-out law candidates at AP 0.133 vs 0.045 heuristic, ~2× more useful laws per episode (adrs/0005).
  • The RL mixture collapse: a diagnosed reward-scale bug was fixed (a per-episode standardized advantage), but the mixture still collapses — a shared-net winner-take-all under a sparse reward, not a scale bias (retros/stage7-multifamily-results.md).

The pattern we extract: learning beats baselines where paying moves are dense; it fails where the paying move is composed or sparse. That is a board-design lesson, not a learner-capacity one.

3. Process lessons that cost real time ​

3.1 Scoped gates missing cross-cutting regressions — three instances ​

We run a heavy gate suite (full pytest, ty, ruff, vulture, import-linter, bandit, semgrep, a radon ratchet, 100 % coverage). Three times a gate went green while a real regression lived outside its scope. The pattern is the same each time: the gate's scope (a package, a corpus, a feature contract) was narrower than the regression's scope.

(1) The unconsumed ports. Four evaluation ports (Profiler, PerformanceModel, Policy, and the ProgramFeatures seam) shipped typed, tested and green — and no code in packages/ consumed them. Every gate passed, because no gate catches an unconsumed port. Coverage counts lines executed by tests, not lines reached by the pipeline. The fix was a rule, now in ADR 0003: "a port is not done until something reaches it" — "wired" is a deliverable of its own.

(2) A correctness fix silently invalidated a learned policy. A view/fold-aware traffic-accounting fix in catopt_core.features (a fix that was right) altered the state input of an RL policy and re-introduced a family collapse in a policy whose documented benefit had previously been recorded — with no change to the RL code. The features tests passed (features changed as intended); the RL tests passed (the RL code was untouched); the documented benefit was silently gone. The cause was found only by an audit for a different bug class and by reverting features.py to an earlier commit and reproducing the old table exactly. The rule that came out of it: a policy is coupled to the semantics of its features — any change to ProgramFeatures must be treated as a change to every policy trained on it (retrain, or version the contract). ADR 0003.

(3) Shipped laws unsound off-corpus. The weight-merge law (x@a + x@b → x@(a+b)) rewrote a real, evaluable term into a numerically different one (max diff 12.83). All four members of the equivalence class shared the hole. select_mul was latently unsound (broadcasting mul along the selected axis changes the result). The corpus could never surface either: every real exported site was in the true region (the 13 real select_mul sites all had shape(u)[D] == shape(v)[D]). The corpus-scoped gates — including sink.verify, which only ever saw the true region — were green. Both were caught only by an adversarial witness generator that synthesizes bindings the corpus does not contain (retros/matmul-unsound-fix.md, retros/select-mul-broadcast.md).

The general lesson, now a rule: "verified on all observed sites" is not "sound." The adversarial layer is the part of the referee that keeps earning its keep, and every shipped law that lacks a cond should eventually get a synthesis pass over its metavar bindings.

3.2 Three host OOMs and what caused them ​

The dev host is an 11 GB / 16-core box (RTX 2050, 4 GB VRAM). Memory is the binding constraint on this project, and we killed the host three times.

(1) The quartic dspec_synth sweep. The conv+BN fold probe's depth-3 dspec_synth sweep over the fold's seven-attr bank is quartic in leaf count. It OOM-killed the process (measured: MemoryError under a 4 GB cap after ~17 s; an OS OOM-kill without one) — and the reboot it caused meant the probe work had to be salvaged from the wreckage (commit 40ea395, "salvaged from the OOM reboot"). The fix routes every fit through dspec_audit.bounded_synth (same candidate space, deepest level streamed with early bail under a wall-clock budget). The general class: unbounded candidate sweeps. retros/weightspace-fold.md.

(2) The arena oracle's unbounded viewed-binding enumeration. The oracle's _synth_bases materialized one _LazySeq per binding — a 9-ary kernel fold over a deep view chain opened ~10^13 rows and OOM-killed every run. Fixed by capping at _MAX_VIEWED_BINDINGS, and by running rows subprocess-isolated with a 7 GB ulimit + 10-min timeout so a wedged row is recorded rather than silently killed. The dead rows (one OOM, one timeout) are kept in the results table honestly. retros/arena-player.md.

(3) The pytest -n auto inductor fan-out. The suite is single-process by design: the torch.compile-heavy tests spawn an inductor pool of 16 compile workers per pytest process, so -n N multiplies the fan-out to ~16·N subprocesses. On the 11 GB/16-core host, -n auto OOM-froze the machine; even -n 3 drove memory to 10.2 GB near the suite's tail. The warning is now a block in AGENTS.md and a commit (6c00529).

A fourth, related datum: the discovery tools carry their own budgets because of this host — a 240 s SIGALRM per-model wall clock, a 3000 s total sweep budget, and a 1.5 GB MemAvailable abort floor (retros/discovery-scale.md); and plan 0024 records "no comm/assoc in default saturation (measured OOM at bounded budgets on the 11 GB host)." The budget discipline is not caution for its own sake; it is the scar tissue.

3.3 The boot-time checklist the recurring agent deaths produced ​

We did not lose work to the OOMs only; long-running agent sessions died mid-task (host freezes, session gaps), and we lost uncommitted work more than once. What emerged — partly written down in AGENTS.md, partly in the tools' conventions — is the checklist we now run at the start of any non-trivial session. There is no single committed checklist file; this is the reconstruction, with the source of each item:

  1. Read AGENTS.md first — it is the boot document. It names the package layout, the "no catopt façade" rule, the 11 machine-admitted laws and the authorship split, and the full gate list. A change to any default rule changes extraction behavior for every caller, so run the full suite, not a slice, when touching the law bank.
  2. Never run the suite under pytest -n. (OOM #3, above.) Use a scoped coverage run -m pytest tests/test_<file>.py for iteration; the full coverage gate is a commit-time check (~20–25 min serial).
  3. Budget every sweep. Any tool that enumerates candidates gets a wall-clock budget, a memory abort floor, and (for row-parallel work) subprocess isolation with a ulimit + timeout. (OOMs #1, #2.)
  4. Commit early and often, per work item. The retros and the docs/tutorials/course/-style single-path commits exist so a reboot costs one work item, not a session. "Salvaged from the OOM reboot" appears in the log twice.
  5. Write the retro before you close the task. A negative with a named gap is a deliverable; an unrecorded negative is a future repeat.
  6. Do not add suppressions to make a gate pass. No # type: ignore, # noqa, # nosec, # pragma: no cover; fix the code or add a justified, documented config entry. Ratchets tighten, never widen.
  7. Check what the gate does not cover. The unconsumed-port and features→policy lessons (§3.1): green does not mean wired, and green does not mean the documented behavior still holds.

If a future host is larger, items 2–3 relax; items 1, 4–7 are about epistemics, not RAM.

4. The reframings ​

Two reframings matter enough to write down, because both were corrections of how we talked about the work rather than what the code did.

4.1 Rediscovery vs discovery ​

The strongest structural result — the RetNet carrier — is a rediscovery. The extracted program is numerically identical (9.8e-9 fp64) to the paper's published chunkwise duality: "the extracted program IS the paper's chunkwise algorithm, not an approximation of it" (retros/retnet-external-baselines.md). The projection-pairing laws on llama2.c rediscover MergedColumnParallelLinear/QKVParallelLinear. We stopped calling these "discoveries." The contribution there is automatic discovery + certified equivalence + per-shape selection of a known linearization — which is a real contribution, just not the one the word "discovery" implies.

The line between rediscovery and creation is precisely where ADR 0007 draws it: the discovery machinery can mint new spellings (law instances, guards, derives, paying forms) and new operators-as-implementations (genkernel binds a measured elementwise pattern to a generated kernel) — but minting a new semantic atom (an op whose meaning did not exist before) is the harder thing. That is the line the minting stack was built to cross, and the one paying crossing so far is mul_unsq_nat.

4.2 "Not a PyTorch optimizer" ​

For most of the project the self-description was "a PyTorch graph optimizer." That was wrong on two counts.

First, backends are adapters, not the core abstraction. catopt-core is torch-free and import-linter enforces it; a new backend implements the Sink port. We tested this by building a second, non-torch backend (ONNX, both directions) and measuring the boundary: zero edits under catopt-core, fp64 round-trip 1.11e-16 through onnxruntime (retros/onnx-package.md, ADR 0006). PyTorch is one application of the engine. The boundary is measured, not asserted.

Second, and more importantly, the thing we built is a general engine: Catopt discovers better solutions by combining mathematical reasoning, verified transformations, and learned search. Optimization is a game over a structured space of equivalent solutions; RL navigates it; the formal layer preserves correctness; applications and backends are replaceable interfaces. That is the reframing the paper now leads with, and it is the one that finally made the three pillars — mathematical discovery, categorical soundness, learned search — line up with the three measurable questions (novelty, soundness, value).

5. What is still open ​

5.1 The law families (plan 0025, section D) ​

The measured boundary: models whose math is already fused expose nothing to the op-algebra laws. Three families are named and untried, in cost order:

  1. Precision — the bounded lane is built and was a certified negative on BiRefNet for a measured reason (thin dedup). Next target: a model where the dedup is thick (a wide-MLP checkpoint), and weight-space members rather than row-gather ones.
  2. Data movement — hoisting invariant structure (Swin masks recomputed per layer, identical). Compete with Inductor here at your peril; measure before building.
  3. Weight space — conv+BN fusion targets exist (43 BatchNorm2d modules measured in real BiRefNet). Per the no-hardcoding rule the work is derive-or-refuse: feed measured (W,b,μ,σ,γ,β) → (W′,b′) bindings to dspec_synth and let the machinery attempt the fold spec; whatever it cannot express becomes a named grammar gap. The probe already ran this: the fold's structure is derivable given a scale leaf, but the cond DSL has no sqrt/rsqrt and the derive value domain is scalar (int | float), so a per-channel scale is unspellable today. Two ordered blockers, both named (retros/weightspace-fold.md).

5.2 ADR 0008 gate 3 — does search reach loop forms? (closed, negative) ​

This was the last open substrate question; it is now closed, and the answer is negative. Three attempts — a proposing rule, a parametric abstraction over the step, and a sharing-aware extraction — each worked on a control and each failed on the real export: the search never reaches a loop-headed form. The full story, with the numbers, is the paper's §2.2 (../docs/paper_draft.md); the one positive the gate work produced is an extraction defect (greedy per-class extraction is 1.01 % off its own cost model on that workload — corrected 2026-10-15; the 5.06 % figure was a hash-order artifact). So the substrate's discovery value is closed, and the presentation value — uniformity, explicit Δ, the eliminated DAG-vs-tree bug class — must carry ADR 0008 alone (plan 0025 section C). The two named causes that would re-open the gate are in the paper's §2.2; the retros are retros/gate3-loop-proposing-rule.md, retros/gate3-parametric-abstraction.md and retros/gate3-extraction-state-spelling.md.

5.3 The enumeration-ordering blocker ​

The one measured gap between the minted atom and pay (§2.2): the paying fold_emit descriptor is >200 k deep in the round-robin. The fix (feat/enum-order) is in flight. The falsification is explicit: if the canonical seed is reached and the laws still fire nowhere real, the pay-null is semantic — record it and stop chasing the atom.

5.4 The form list and the mint target ​

Plan 0024 measured the form level and the honest reading was narrow: over 12,000 candidates the machine admitted 7 paying laws from forms outside the shipped 27, but each is a composite re-spelling of two shipped identities. "The form list is not the whole grammar, but the forms it lacks are shallow." The open question moves to part B — whether a minted atom (vocabulary, not a re-spelling) can pay where no composition does. Part B's own measurement inverted the plan's assumption: the machine's need-detection backlog named mod (6 rules unblocked), not pow (0 rules) — the plan's guess about which op was missing was wrong, and the machinery said so.

5.5 Scale validation and executor leanness ​

Two things the paper explicitly defers: production dimensions (d ≥ 1024, B ≥ 8, a datacenter GPU, trained linear-attention checkpoints) — because the dev-box magnitudes do not extrapolate — and executor leanness, because per-leaf dispatch is the loss driver and the FLA gap is now precisely attributed there (fused fp32 Triton vs fp64 torch GEMMs + a materialized score tensor).

5.6 Three more open items (carried over from the retired writeup) ​

The retired research writeup named three open items beyond the two above; they are kept here so they are not lost:

  • Fusion-aware extraction. fusion_regions (predicted Inductor kernel count) is landed for reporting; completing it for selection needs either additive decomposition that respects kernel boundaries or more _carrier_upgrade-style coordinated passes.
  • Pipeline scalability. The spine walk + saturation at large T is the compile-time wall (215–262 s at T=2048); incremental matching already took 4-layer models 768 s → 4.9 s, and the same direction is needed for T.
  • Thicker frontiers. Scan-block alternates are near-identical; discrimination currently rides on executor variants, not form diversity.

7. Closing note ​

The most useful thing in this notebook is probably §3. The measurements in §6 are reproducible; the process lessons are what cost us days. If you take one rule away: the gates tell you what they cover, and you must ask what they do not. Every one of the three cross-cutting regressions in §3.1 was green under every gate we had. We found them because we built an adversary that lives outside the corpus, an audit that asked what the feature contract silently carried, and a rule that a port is not done until something reaches it.

And the second rule: write the negative down. The weight-space falsification, the reach-null, the pay-null, the bounded negative — each one is a retro that stopped someone (us, later) from re-running the same experiment hoping for a different answer.
