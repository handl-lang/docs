Retro — the joint board: forward + backward on one e-graph ​

Plan 0021's meta-arena gave the board a declare/handle action space; training.py's reverse handler made gradient programs ordinary terms. joint_probe asks the natural follow-up: the autograd pipeline is two programs (forward graph, backward graph) that can never share work across the boundary — does one e-graph over forward + every gradient root do better?

Reproduce:

.venv/bin/python -m catopt_discovery.training --joint

The measurement ​

Per _demo_forwards case: backward derives every leaf's gradient term under a shaped all-ones cotangent. The separate arm saturates the forward in one EGraph and the whole gradient program in a second, billing the two extracted programs independently — the autograd comparison, where cross-program sharing is impossible. The joint arm adds every root to one e-graph and saturates once; dag_cost dedups shared subtrees, so joint < separate is measured cross-boundary sharing.

count_cost, DEFAULT rules, max_nodes=20k:

forwardseparatejointshared e-classesenodes sep/joint
silu_fwd10.010.0217/15
softmax_fwd13.013.0321/18
linear_fwd13.010.0627/21

linear_fwd wins 23%: tanh's VJP spec spells 1 - tanh(a0)^2, so the entire forward term is a subterm of all three gradient roots — in one e-graph it is one e-class, extracted once, billed once. In two pipelines it is paid twice (once as the forward, once inside each gradient program). Same for matmul(x,w) shared between dx = g·W^T's siblings.

The enodes column is the second win, quieter but unconditional: one saturation over the union costs fewer enodes than two saturations (17→15, 21→18, 27→21) because shared subterms have a shared rewrite frontier.

The honest wrinkle — fusion hides sharing ​

silu/softmax are flat despite 2–3 shared e-classes. Cause: silu_fold/softmax_fold fuse the forward into silu(x) / softmax(x) — locally optimal (−1 op) — but the fused member no longer contains sigmoid(x)/exp(x) as a visible subterm, so the backward's spelled reuse can't dedup against it. Greedy per-class extraction picks the fused member because it prices each class in isolation.

Without the folds (rules=DEFAULT - {silu_fold, softmax_fold}) the joint arm wins both: silu 11→10, softmax 15→13. So the sharing is real — but see below: forcing the spelled member back only ties, so the fold is a wash, not a failure.

The named lever — measured: a wash ​

The codebase already has the mechanism: extract_best's overrides / extract_paired — built for the fused-GEMM-sharing case where k members each selecting split_i(fused) must coordinate to bill fused once. The joint board needs the dual: unfold the forward's fused member to expose a subterm the backward pays for anyway.

Measured, and it ties. Forcing the spelled mul/div member of the forward root (overrides={fwd_class: spelled_enode}) leaves silu at 10 and softmax at 13 — identical to greedy. The math is exact under count_cost: fused silu(x) saves precisely the one op that sharing sigmoid(x) would have saved. For one-use sharing, fusion ≡ sharing and coordination buys nothing.

The structural win is multi-use sharing: linear_fwd's tanh term is consumed by the forward plus all three gradients (4 consumers), so billing it once saves 3 copies — and greedy extraction already captures it because the shared form is the class optimum. The general rule the probe supports: joint saturations pay when a subterm has ≥3 consumers across the boundary; folds and one-use shares are a wash. A cost model that prices launches/memory rather than op count could break the tie differently — flops_cost is the obvious next column.

Caveats ​

  • Extraction correctness is pinned, not assumed: joint-extracted gradient terms evaluate numerically equal to torch.autograd (test_joint_extraction_stays_correct, the linear case — the one that shares hardest).
  • The separate arm is the strong baseline: it bills the gradient program as one DAG (all gradient roots dedup among themselves), which is more than torch actually does at the FX level. Joint still wins or ties everywhere.
  • shared counts e-classes reachable from both the forward and gradient roots — an upper bound on exploitable sharing, not all of it billable under greedy extraction (see the wrinkle).
  • Demo corpus is three hand-shaped cases (silu, softmax spine, linear+tanh). The claim needs a real module sweep next — TorchSource already produces such terms (the autograd end-to-end test does this for backward alone).
