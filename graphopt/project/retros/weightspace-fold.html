Retro — the weight-space lane: the conv+BN fold, derived or refused ​

Date: 2026-10-14. Tool: lab/probes/weightspace_fold_probe.py (merged f83928d). Depends on: plan 0025 (the missing law families), plan 0012 (bounded rewrites), ADR 0003.

Why this lane ​

The measured boundary from this cycle: models whose math is already fused expose nothing to the op-algebra laws (BiRefNet delivered FLOP-neutral). Three families were named as the expansion frontier; weight space is the one that tests the project's no-hardcoding rule — the fold must be derived by the machinery or refused with a named gap, never hand-written.

The fold is a pure identity on parameters:

W'[o] = W[o]·γ[o]/√(σ²[o] + ε)      b' = (b − μ)·γ/√(σ² + ε) + β

In IR terms it is a derived-leaf rewrite: the RHS weights are computed from the LHS's leaves. Real BiRefNet's decoder carries 43 nn.BatchNorm2d modules (birefnet-bounded-lane.md) and no conv+BN law ships.

The probe's gate-by-gate account (measured) ​

gateverdict
1 · binding source✅ the fold is a measured parameter identity — Conv2d+BatchNorm2d (eval): `max
2 · dspec_synth fit⚠️ raw fold: no fit (W' None, b' None at depth 2–3). Pre-scaled: it fits — W' = mul(W, scale), and at depth 3 b' = add(beta, mul(scale, sub(b, mu))). So the fold's structure is derivable given a scale leaf; the missing piece is the derivation of scale = γ/√(σ²+ε)
3 · cond DSL❌ no sqrt, no rsqrt; float-exponent pow declines (fdiv/recip are fine) — so the DSL cannot spell scale
4 · derive value domain❌ the derive value domain is **`int
5 · e-graph visibility✅/❌ the IR does carry the node (batch_norm × 2 in the census), but no law LHS names it across 426 rules — nothing can rewrite it
6 · cost model⚠️ batch_norm is absent from the FLOPs table (default price 256.0); sqrt/rsqrt are present
7 · derived-leaf seam✅ exists, is procedural, and registers source tensors

The verdict: refused, with the gap named precisely ​

The fold is structurally derivable — the synthesizer finds the correct two-expression form once a scale leaf is supplied, which is strong evidence the machinery understands the rewrite's shape. It cannot be completed for two independent, measured reasons:

  1. The cond/derive DSL has no square root (sqrt/rsqrt absent; float pow declines) — scale = γ/√(σ²+ε) is unspellable.
  2. The derive value domain is scalar (int | float) — a per-channel tensor scale cannot be computed as a derived value, even if the DSL gained an rsqrt.

Plus two gaps that are content, not capability, and are therefore the parts a human may legitimately author:

  1. No law LHS names batch_norm — the rewrite does not exist in the library (this is what the derived law would be).
  2. batch_norm is unpriced in the FLOPs table, so the fold would not be ranked even if it existed.

What this buys the project ​

  • The no-hardcoding principle held under test: the machine derived the fold's structure and refused the rest with named causes — rather than a human writing conv_bn_fold and calling it done.
  • A concrete, ordered backlog: (a) tensor-valued derive leaves (the source_tensors seam exists but the value domain does not admit them), (b) rsqrt/sqrt in the derive DSL, (c) batch_norm in the FLOPs table, (d) the law itself — derivable once (a)+(b) land.
  • An OOM class found and fixed: the probe's depth-3 dspec_synth sweep over the fold's seven-attr bank is quartic in leaf count and OOM-killed the process (measured: MemoryError under a 4 GB cap after ~17 s; an OS OOM-kill without one). Routing every fit through dspec_audit.bounded_synth (same candidate space, deepest level streamed with early bail under a wall-clock budget) makes it run end to end — the same class of unbounded-sweep risk the discovery work has hit before.

Honest limits ​

  • Measured on CPU, small shapes (W(6,4,3,3), W(6,8)), fp64 — the identity is what is being verified, not a speedup.
  • The probe refuses rather than derives: this retro records a negative, and the negative is the result.
  • No pay was measured, because nothing could be admitted — the two blockers above come first.
