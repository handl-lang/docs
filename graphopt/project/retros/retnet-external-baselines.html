Retro: RetNet carrier vs external baselines ​

Date: session of 2026-10-14. Probe: lab/probes/retnet_baseline_probe.py (committed 6eca509). Dev box: RTX 2050-class GPU, fp64 unless noted.

Why this exists ​

The README's "7.30x less memory" row was measured against our own spelled-quadratic _ChunkedRetention reference — not against the RetNet paper's implementation or tuned third-party kernels. This probe re-measures the delivered K-way carrier against external baselines. Verdict below is honest: the carrier is the paper's algorithm, and the hand-tuned world still beats our artifact.

Arms ​

  • spelled-eager / spelled-compiled — the optimize-input model.
  • carrier (fp64) / carrier32 (fp32) — extracted + lowered K-way carrier.
  • paper-eqn — faithful in-script reimplementation of Sun et al.'s chunkwise recurrence, gamma matched (0.9), no stability rescale.
  • paper-code — official torchscale chunk_recurrent_forward driven directly (inner_mask inlined verbatim from RetNetRelPos.forward, decay patched to log(0.9)).
  • fla-chunk / fla-rec — fla-org chunk_retention / fused_recurrent_retention, fp32 (Triton).

Numbers (T=2048, K=8, C=256) ​

spelled-eager     169.92 MiB   21.73 ms   1.00x
spelled-compiled   73.90 MiB   27.01 ms   2.30x
carrier            30.01 MiB    3.14 ms   5.66x
paper-eqn          26.91 MiB    2.12 ms   6.32x
paper-code         27.44 MiB    3.00 ms   6.19x
carrier32          19.45 MiB    2.38 ms   8.73x
fla-chunk           9.96 MiB    0.28 ms  17.05x
fla-rec            10.15 MiB    1.16 ms  16.74x

T=512 / T=1024 same ordering; carrier loses at 512, crosses ~1024.

Numerics ​

  • carrier vs spelled-eager: 9.8e-9 fp64 — the delivery is correct.
  • carrier vs paper-eqn: 9.8e-9 fp64 — the extracted program IS the paper's chunkwise algorithm, not an approximation of it.
  • carrier vs paper-code: ~0.5 — the official impl applies an all_scale row-normalization (fp16 stability) that is NOT an exact identity; its output differs from raw retention by construction. Worth knowing if anyone compares against torchscale outputs.

What this means for the claims ​

  1. "Beats its own input" — yes, decisively (5.66x memory, 6.9x speed at T=2048; 2.5x memory and ~8.6x speed vs the input under torch.compile — compile fixes fusion, not asymptotics).
  2. "Beats the paper's chunkwise impl" — no: ~1.1x more memory, ~1.5x slower than a plain hand-written chunked loop.
  3. "Beats tuned kernels" — no: FLA is ~3x less memory and ~11x faster. That gap is lowering quality — our artifact is a general lowering of the extracted term (many small ops, materialized decay vectors), not a fused scan kernel.
  4. "A new transformation" — no: it is the paper's published duality, machine-rederived and machine-verified.

The defensible claim stands at: certified mechanical reachability of a published linearization + delivery as a verified runnable module. Closing the FLA gap is a lowering/kernel problem (genkernel/fusion), not a semantics problem — a separate dimension by ADR 0003.
