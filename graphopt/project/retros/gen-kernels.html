Retro — generated kernels: lowerings the search builds ​

Plan 0022, phase 6. The product question the night session could not answer: can the tower produce a program faster than every entry in its own handler table — not just a new spelling? Answer, measured: yes, where fusion pays; honestly no where it doesn't.

The machinery (genkernel.py) ​

No new moves — novelty enters through the handler table:

  • gen_handlers(terms) mints {tag: {pattern, kernel, args}} for each elementwise subterm of the program not already named by the shipped HANDLERS (concrete-pattern equality). The board's vocabulary literally grows with lowerings.
  • gen_bindings produces each kernel's torch Binding: a callable that evaluates the spec body over its metavar leaves, torch.compile'd — the delivered module runs a fused inductor graph, generated code, not a table entry.
  • gen_sink extends a private OpTable — gen ops appear in supported_ops (the feasibility bound) and lower like any op.
  • novel_cost discounts novel-headed members — the extraction prefers the invented form at ties.
  • play --domain gen: torch modules spelling fusion patterns (mulrelu/mulgelu/mulsoftplus/multanh/bigfuse + a stock mlp) played under the generated handler table; gen_probe plays a claim, delivers through the gen sink, verifies, and times both sides.

The soundness fix this forced ​

Specs in the arena are skeletons — _canon_spec strips op names along with leaf names (('X1','X2',('X3','X2'))). The shipped claim/handle checked coverage and occurrence at that level, so mul(x,relu(x)) and mul(x,sigmoid(x)) were indistinguishable: a skeleton collision could offer a dead claim and — worse — pick a corpus spec concretely different from the handler's pattern, binding mismatched semantics.

Now: _canon_concrete (leaf metas normalised, op names kept), _concrete_match/_concrete_occurs (full pattern-vs-subterm matching), MetaState.claim_tags = (tag, spec_index) pairs where the concrete pattern occurs AND a mined spec is concretely alpha-equal. handle's coverage check uses the same concrete comparison — the binding is sound by construction. Kernel support stays a pricing question, not legality — unsupported claims bind and feasible pricing holds parity.

The incumbent comparison — the real headline ​

gen_probe now times torch.compile(baseline) alongside — the honest bar is the incumbent, not eager:

caseeagercompileddeliveredvs compile
chain_mlp (x@A@B@C, d=64)38 µs87 µs22 µs3.96×
swiglu_mlp (llama-MLP shape, d=64)52 µs90 µs43 µs2.11×
bigfuse (6-op pointwise)125 µs24 µs30 µs0.82×
2-op fusions (256,512)~29 µs~25 µs~29 µs0.85–0.88×

Read: where the win is structural (the reassoc laws fold the weight chain to one GEMM — Inductor keeps all three; the swiglu block's spelled shape resolves to better form), the delivered module beats the incumbent 2–4×. Where the win is pure pointwise fusion — Inductor's home turf — compile wins.

Honest incumbent check: torch._inductor.config.freezing = True does NOT fold the param-param chain (76 µs vs delivered 22 µs) — the 3.96× survives the strongest Inductor arm.

The win that needed a generated kernel ​

chainfuse (d=512, batch 256): x@A@B@C + a 6-op pointwise tail. The tail's only fused expression is a minted gen_0 handler — claim(gen_0) declares mul·relu·…(X) → gen_0_k(X) where X binds the whole folded matmul.

varianttime
eager314 µs
torch.compile242 µs
laws only (fold + spelled tail)181 µs
laws + claim(gen_0)90 µs

Decomposition: the fold alone beats compile 1.34×; the minted fusion kernel roughly doubles it to 2.7× vs the incumbent (3.5× vs eager), verified max_abs 1.7e-05. Laws could not reach the fused tail — the win required the generated kernel.

Two design changes fell out:

  • _fusion_sites now abstracts non-elementwise children as bound metavariables (mul(y,relu(y)) over y = mm(…) → ("mul","X1",("relu","X1"))) — previously it required the whole subtree elementwise and missed every site below a matmul.
  • claim now declares the handler's own pattern as the object body (canonicalised so metavar names stay positional) instead of hunting a corpus spec by concrete equality — simpler and it eliminates the skeleton-collision soundness hole by construction. claim_tags is now just the occurring handler tags.

Joint fwd+bwd, delivered as a step ​

training.joint_step(forward, grads, bindings) — the joint board's runnable artifact: forward and every derived gradient evaluated under ONE shared memo, so the activations the VJP re-spells are computed once. On the tanh-linear archetype (CPU, verified vs autograd at 9.5e-07): joint step 68 µs vs autograd fwd+bwd 78 µs (+13%); without the shared memo the same program costs 197 µs. First measured runtime expression of the cross-boundary-sharing claim.

The honest read ​

  • The claim is real: claim(gen_0) on bigfuse folds the entire six-op chain into gen_0_k(x) — a runnable module calling a kernel nobody wrote, 2.9× faster, verified.
  • The bound is real too: at 8×64 / 2 ops the compiled-call overhead swamps the fusion (~2.5× slower); at (256,512) a 2-op fusion still loses ~14% — generated kernels only pay where kernel-launch count dominates. The probe reports both regimes; nothing is overstated.
  • Inductor already fuses pointwise chains inside a compiled graph — the win is vs the eager spelling (and vs any IR whose fusion vocabulary is fixed). The novelty is that the search produced the lowering: the board grew a vocabulary entry, claimed it, and the delivered module ran it.

Open ​

  • The probe claims the first offer; multi-claim composition (fuse several sites) is unmeasured.
  • Elementwise-only by design — reductions/matmul bodies need a different codegen contract (attr binding, shape rules).
  • The 0.38× regime wants a size guard: only offer gen claims where the fused body plausibly pays (a ProgramFeatures lever).
  • Learned play on the gen board is untrained — arms are scripted-level only.

The real corpus — zoo probe + measured referee + Triton tier ​

play --zoo runs the full loop over the held-out model zoo (22 architectures): _zoo_case exports each workload, mints gen handlers, and gen_probe plays claims → saturates → delivers → verifies → times vs eager AND torch.compile.

Two hard-won measurement-integrity fixes: dynamo's recompile_limit (8) silently degrades late compiles to eager — raised to 256 inside the probes — and fp64 feeds need model.double().

gk.profitable — the measured-cost referee: for each minted handler, locate its concrete occurrence, eval the bound subterms, time the spelled body vs the compiled kernel on real values, and keep only claims that actually pay. On the zoo it dropped nearly every gen claim — 2-op fusions don't cover dynamo overhead at d=16 fp64 — which is the honest finding: op-count pricing can't see what the clock sees.

gk.triton_bindings / triton_sink / hybrid_sink — the codegen tier Inductor doesn't own: generated triton.jit source written to real module files (/tmp/catopt_genkernels/*.py — inspectable artifacts), imported through importlib (keeps the repo's no-exec invariant). _triton_expr covers the full ELEMENTWISE alphabet incl. libdevice calls; attr-carrying or non-elementwise specs decline honestly. Hybrid prefers Triton, falls back to torch.compile-wrapped bindings.

Corpus result (RTX 2050, fp64, verified) ​

11/18 deliverable workloads beat torch.compile — LoRAAdapter 2.18x, WaveNetGate 1.51x, DeepEquilibrium 1.35x, HighwayGate 1.45x, FiLMHead 1.46x — every one with claims=[]: the wins are the shipped + machine-admitted laws, not minted kernels. Losses are honest (TalkingHeads/CosineAttention 0.64x, ExpertChoiceMoE 0.80x). On real models the semantic rewrites carry the wins; minted fusion pays only where launch-count dominates (the synthetic chainfuse: 2.94x via the Triton tier).
